Abstract

Robotic imitation learning typically requires policies that capture multimodal action distributions, run at real-time control rates, and accommodate multiple sensing modalities. Recent generative approaches—diffusion, flow matching, IMLE—each satisfy a subset of these requirements but not all three.

We present PRISM, a single-pass multisensory policy that meets all three by operationalizing a batch-global variant of RS–IMLE. A key technical observation: the RS–IMLE rejection mechanism, designed for few-shot image synthesis, silently fails at policy-scale batch sizes. We identify why and provide the calibration that restores it.

On real-world loco-manipulation (Unitree GO2 + 7-DoF arm) and tabletop manipulation (UR5), PRISM outperforms diffusion policies by 10–25 pp in success while sustaining 30–50 Hz closed-loop control. On CALVIN (full ABCD, language-conditioned), it improves success by ~31 pp over diffusion and ~24 pp over flow matching while reducing trajectory jerk by 20×–70×.

Language & audio — vision-conditioned manipulation.

§ 02  /  Loco-Manipulation

Vision & tactile contact-rich manipulation on a quadruped.

PRISM running on a Unitree GO2 with a 7-DoF D1 arm. Tactile feedback from the gripper is fused with wrist and shoulder RGB at every timestep, enabling robust contact-rich behaviors that pure vision baselines fail to recover.

§ 03  /  Pre-Manipulation

Whole-body parking for a 14-DoF locomotion + manipulation policy.

Before contact-rich manipulation can begin, the GO2 base must reach a stable, kinematically reachable pose. PRISM’s 14-D action space (gripper + EE pose + base linear & angular velocity) lets a single policy plan the approach end-to-end from RGB observations.
